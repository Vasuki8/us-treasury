## Unit and alignment contract

For a single exact reference quarter `q`:

```text
quarterly nominal GDP(q) = nominal GDP SAAR(q) / 4
current-account-to-GDP(q), percent
    = 100 × current-account quarterly balance(q) / (nominal GDP SAAR(q) / 4)
    = 400 × current-account quarterly balance(q) / nominal GDP SAAR(q)
```

This follows BEA's [annual-rates definition](https://www.bea.gov/help/glossary/annual-rates): quarterly dollar levels become annual rates through multiplication by four. The [ITA Table 1.1 notes](https://apps.bea.gov/iTable/?isuri=1&product=1&reqid=62&step=6&tablelist=1) explicitly identify quarterly ITA estimates as not annualized. Dividing the quarterly balance directly by GDP SAAR would understate the magnitude by four. The division by four here is a dollar-level conversion; it is unrelated to compounding or deannualizing GDP **growth** rates.

Match the same quarter-start reference date in both admitted quarterly series. Do not substitute a previous quarter, interpolate GDP, use real/chained-dollar GDP, use a growth rate, or join to a nearby release date. Null, absent, nonfinite or nonpositive GDP denominators remain unavailable. Missing or nonfinite numerators remain unavailable; an observed zero numerator is a valid zero ratio. Preserve the numerator's sign: positive is a current-account surplus, negative is a deficit. A change in the ratio is expressed in **percentage points**, not percentage growth. The ratio is not itself an annualized growth rate or a trailing-year ratio.

The current-account balance includes goods, services, primary income and secondary income. NIPA trade/net exports omit income and transfers and use national-account adjustments. Do not substitute NIPA trade balance or claim the current account is an exact GDP component. This is a size comparison between a balance-of-payments flow and domestic production, not an accounting reconciliation or causal explanation.

## Balance changes versus GDP-denominator effects

Rechecked 23 October 2026. This extension reuses the same admitted commercial BEA statistical scope and endpoints; it adds a Meridian accounting convention, not a new official dataset. [BEA public-domain guidance](https://www.bea.gov/help/faq/147), [citation guidelines](https://www.bea.gov/help/guidelines-for-citing-bea), and [annual-rate units](https://www.bea.gov/help/glossary/annual-rates) continue to apply. No source refresh or permission/credential dependency is introduced.

Method `symmetric-two-order-v1`: average changing the balance first and GDP first, allocating their interaction equally. With prior/current signed balances N0/N1 and matching positive GDP flows D0/D1, balance effect = `50 × (N1−N0) × (1/D0+1/D1)` and GDP-denominator effect = `50 × (N0+N1) × (1/D1−1/D0)`, in percentage points. Their sum reconciles to the original ratio change within floating precision. Apply separately to total, components, component sum and the raw published-total-minus-components balance residual. Component effects plus residual effects reconcile to total effects before rounding. Existing combined contributions already contain GDP effects; these new columns split those contributions rather than add another term to them. Other order conventions can assign interaction differently. This is not an official BEA contribution, unique observed attribution or causal explanation.

For quarterly changes use each quarter's balance and GDP SAAR/4. For T4Q compare two adjacent windows' separately summed balances and matching quarterly GDP flows; do not average quarterly splits. Five raw consecutive quarters support the adjacent overlapping windows. Keep pre-filter predecessor lookup, independent missing reasons, finite intermediate/cross-ratio guards and no older-date fallback. Missing components can leave the total split available; full residual reconciliation requires every component plus the total at both endpoints. A positive GDP effect on a negative balance means mechanical narrowing relative to GDP, not an improvement in its dollar balance.

At retained 2026Q2 versus Q1, quarterly total balance effect is −0.4148264943973819 pp and GDP effect +0.05798105789038233 pp; T4Q effects are +0.028118301209965637 pp and +0.04601340585733303 pp. Differences of approximately 1e−15 pp from the original total/component reconciliation arise from floating arithmetic, not an invented economic residual.
