# Fiscal pressure source review

Reviewed 21 October 2026 for Meridian, `Vasuki8/us-treasury`; debt historical coverage reverified 25 October 2026. Scope is factual Treasury fiscal/debt statistics, their calculated metrics and downloadable CSVs. Agency seals, copied narrative, photographs and third-party material are excluded.

## Commercial admission

[Treasury Fiscal Data's License and Authorization](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/api-documentation/#license-and-authorization) explicitly allows commercial use, copying, adaptation and redistribution without restriction. The page was fetched directly with HTTP 200 during this review; the search browser's separate 403 was not treated as an unavailable license. This is the existing repository's Fiscal Data admission basis, extended to these exact official dataset endpoints. Retain Treasury/Bureau of the Fiscal Service attribution, source URLs and retrieval provenance; label application calculations independently. No account, API key, paid service or new infrastructure is needed.

## Exact endpoints and fields

All paths below follow `https://api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov/services/api/fiscal_service`. Live API responses returned HTTP 200. Each capture uses `requestText`, a SHA256 of exact response bytes, real retrieval time and original URL. Redirects and incomplete monthly-table pagination are rejected. The [October 26 historical-gap audit](../historical-gap-audit.md) replaces the original moving six-year MTS window with the common native table history from **March 2015** through the capture date. This restores the January–November 2020 trailing totals/ratio from actual earlier inputs; the first 11 newly retained months still lack a full annual window. Both the main gross-debt and fiscal composition collectors request debt history from `1993-04-01` through the same explicit capture cutoff, using the same admitted Debt to the Penny endpoint. Only actual record dates are retained.

Debt captures remain bounded to a single 10,000-row page. Both collectors require numeric response count, total-count and total-pages metadata proving that every requested row is present, and reject a next-page link, mismatched counts, absent metadata or more than one page. The [official API documentation](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/api-documentation/) defines those pagination fields. This fails closed if future growth exceeds the bound, preserving the previous successful snapshot instead of silently dropping older dates. On 25 October 2026, the explicitly bounded official response contained 8,419 rows from 1 April 1993 through 22 October 2026, with `count=total-count=8419`, `total-pages=1` and no next link. No new provider, endpoint, pagination receipt or source license was introduced; each full response retains its own exact URL, hash and actual capture time.

## Coverage, cadence and dates

[Monthly Treasury Statement](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/monthly-treasury-statement/) is monthly. The metadata service lists table 2 and table 9 coverage from 31 March 2015, and the dedicated monthly receipts/outlays series from 31 October 1980; financing in that longer series is not readily available before January 2010. Current latest reference month at review: September 2026. Table 9/table 2 `record_date` values are month ends, although the generic metadata definition calls them publication dates. Their actual September values have `record_date=2026-09-30` and were fetched in October; this field must not be represented as the actual release instant. `publishedAt` stays null. Reference period, retrieval time and source update metadata are distinct.

[Debt to the Penny](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/debt-to-the-penny/) updates at the end of each business day for the preceding business day. Gross debt coverage begins 1 April 1993. Public/intragovernmental breakdown is annual from 30 September 1997 through September 2001, monthly through March 2005, then daily from 4 April 2005. The official dataset warns that unpublished historical composition can appear as paired `$0.00` placeholders; the reviewed JSON instead returned literal `"null"` strings on the earliest dates. Both representations stay null in calculated public and intragovernmental series before daily reporting began. Gross debt remains available on those dates and all original strings remain retained. Sparse annual/monthly published composition is kept only on its actual reported dates; no interpolation or forward filling is performed. Unexpected missing modern composition still fails closed. Debt held by the public includes Federal Reserve Banks and foreign holders, and is not limited to private or domestic investors. Debt-to-Penny intragovernmental holdings include Federal Financing Bank securities, which can differ from other Treasury datasets' coverage.

No release dates, historical first-release dates or historical publication instants are invented. Store immutable retrieval snapshots going forward; an initial retrieval does not prove what was known at an earlier date.

## Units and sample checks

There is a material documentation inconsistency: generic MTS descriptions say all values are millions, but actual current-month table 2/table 9 JSON values are full dollars with cents. For September 2026, table 9 receipts are `557874519238.24`, outlays `584774866340.26`, net interest `34787531902.86`. The dedicated explicitly labeled million series reports receipts `557875` and outlays `584775`. The adapter checks the latest common report against both series within `$500,001`, reflecting nearest-million rounding plus a dollar of floating-point allowance. An impossible scale or absent latest common check fails closed; historical actuals are converted by `/1e9` to USD billions. Budget estimates such as `5475705.00` are not used.

As of 20 October 2026, Debt to the Penny reported gross debt `40439546016039.08`, public debt `32566198492042.62`, and intragovernmental debt `7873347523996.46` dollars. These three current values reconcile. The raw strings for all three measures are retained in the supplemental debt breakdown, alongside their calculated trillion-dollar values. Do not infer more precision in on-screen rounded displays than the calculation/export states.

The first gross observation is `4225873987843.44` dollars on 1 April 1993. The first published composition is 30 September 1997: public debt `3789667546849.60`, intragovernmental debt `1623478464547.74`, gross debt `5413146011397.34` dollars. These values reconcile and retain their exact source strings, including trailing cents.

The full 8,419-row live review on 25 October 2026 found one published composition anomaly: on 4 August 2025, public plus intragovernmental debt exceeds published gross debt by $10 billion. The original fiscal capture quarantined that composition row while the main series retained the published gross figure. The [subsequent official-source review](debt-2025-08-04-source-review.md) verified the dated Daily Treasury Statement's FFB-inclusive closing balances and their next-day opening counterparts. The user approved that whole-row fallback: public `29523300`, intragovernmental `7314865`, gross `36838165`, each **USD millions, rounded to the nearest million**. Both collectors use this same row only for the exact reviewed primary conflict and exact verified PDF bytes. Original primary strings, signed residual, both actual receipts and explicit source precision remain in each repaired observation and exports; no corrected cents are inferred. A reconciled primary record takes precedence. Changed or unavailable PDF evidence cannot admit an unverified replacement. Other material accounting discrepancies remain quarantined, and all earlier immutable captures retain their original values and unavailable rows. Documented missing historical composition receives a separate `composition-not-published` marker and is not mislabeled as an accounting discrepancy. The review found no missing or zero public/intragovernmental amounts from 4 April 2005 onward.

## Calculations and interpretation

- Positive deficit = outlays minus receipts. Table 2's budget balance has the opposite sign (surplus positive, deficit negative); table 1 and the dedicated longer series use deficit positive.
- Primary deficit = deficit minus published **net budget interest**, using aligned month inputs. Gross Treasury debt-interest expense is a different measure and is not substituted for missing net interest.
- A trailing 12-month total requires exactly 12 consecutive nonmissing monthly observations. Never add FYTD values across months or reset a trailing window in October.
- Net-interest-to-revenue uses aligned net interest / receipts, preferably trailing totals to avoid tax-payment seasonality; missing or zero-denominator inputs remain unavailable.
- Contributor changes describe accounting components of the observed change, not proven economic causes. Offset receipts and negative outlays can offset increases. Preserve a residual if source components do not add exactly to totals, rather than forcing a match.
- The financing bridge is deficit = borrowing from the public + reduction of operating cash + other means + calculated source residual. A cash increase is a negative reduction. Historical source financing discrepancies are retained explicitly: 26 months in 2020–2023 have a residual exceeding $1, with the largest reviewed discrepancy about minus $2.890 million in July 2021. Current September 2026 reconciles. No source amount is changed to clear the residual.
- Deficit is a period flow. Gross debt and debt held by the public are stocks with different scopes. Cash balances, other financing, intragovernmental activity and debt-accounting details prevent equating a deficit to a change in total outstanding debt.

Historical current-month rows are **as recorded in each monthly statement; later revisions may not be incorporated**. Example: the August 2026 original table 9 outlay differs from the August comparative shown in September table 1. This milestone does not pretend those series are interchangeable or reconstruct missing historical vintages.
