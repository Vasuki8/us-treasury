# Expectations, financial conditions and market-implied policy: source decisions

Reviewed 27 October 2026 Eastern. This is a bounded feasibility review; it does not claim that no possible provider exists. The app's requirements include unattended collection, commercial public display, persisted public captures, exports and frozen-package redistribution, without creating a new account, subscription or infrastructure service.

## Release expectations alongside actual/prior values

The [Trading Economics calendar documentation](https://docs.tradingeconomics.com/economic_calendar/) exposes calendar APIs and [authentication](https://docs.tradingeconomics.com/get_started/authentication/) for structured release context. Its [calendar response fields](https://docs.tradingeconomics.com/economic_calendar/schema/) distinguish actual, previous, revised and forecast fields. A numerically populated website does not establish this application's rights to retain and republish the provider's consensus database. No user-owned credentials or commercial redistribution grant for that service are configured or established by this review; no trial/sample/third-party key is used. The exact dependency is an admitted provider agreement permitting automated access, public storage, downstream CSV/package redistribution and sufficient pre-release timestamps/version history to distinguish original prior, revised prior and as-of consensus. Authentication alone would not settle redistribution or historic as-of semantics.

The [Philadelphia Fed Survey of Professional Forecasters](https://www.philadelphiafed.org/surveys-and-data/real-time-data-research/survey-of-professional-forecasters) is a primary quarterly survey with downloadable mean/median forecasts and survey dates. It is potentially useful as a separately labeled quarterly survey, but it is not a release-calendar consensus for each upcoming monthly Employment Situation, CPI or PCE release. Its forecast definitions/horizons would require a separate exact mapping and reuse review; its presence does not fill absent pre-release consensus fields. Cleveland inflation nowcasts and model projections similarly remain distinct from consensus. Scheduled calendar dates, today's revised levels and first-captured values cannot substitute for original actual/revised-prior publication evidence.

Decision: no expectation/surprise panel or placeholder is shipped. The existing publication archives and captured-change tools continue to identify their own evidence precisely. The Employment Situation publication limitation is separately documented in `labor-inflation-evidence-source-review.md`.

## Modest financial and credit context already present

The existing [credit/policy source review](credit-policy-context-source-review.md) admits Board H.8 bank loans/deposits, SLOOS standards/demand, H.4.1 balances and New York Fed overnight operations with exact source definitions and rights. `src/credit-context-page.js` already provides native charts, definitions, selected-window CSV and frozen-source integration. These are useful modest credit/funding conditions: stocks, respondent balances and overnight operations remain separate measurements. They are not misrepresented as corporate bond spreads, a broad conditions index or a forecast. This portion is already implemented and requires no additional proprietary market dataset.

The [Chicago Fed NFCI page](https://www.chicagofed.org/research/data/nfci/current-data) describes a broader weekly index constructed from 105 indicators. This would be an additional composite with its own methodology, revisable history and third-party-input/reuse review; it is unnecessary to duplicate the existing modest scoped credit context in this change. No unreviewed NFCI observations or synthetic composite are admitted.

## Market-implied policy pricing

The [CME FedWatch page](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/interest-rates/cme-fedwatch-tool) identifies its probabilities as derived from 30-day federal-funds futures. The [official API page](https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data/market-data-api.html) directs FedWatch API access through CME's service portal, while its [licensing page](https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data/license-data.html) distinguishes internal display, non-display, distribution and derived products. Media attribution on a public tool page does not establish blanket automated database retention and downstream redistribution rights. No account or license is acquired.

The [Atlanta Fed Market Probability Tracker](https://www.atlantafed.org/research-and-data/data/market-probability-tracker) is a serious first-party alternative, with historical data and model documentation. Its quantities concern the three-month compounded SOFR distribution inferred from CME options, rather than an exact FOMC-meeting federal-funds path. The page explicitly identifies CME permission for the Atlanta Fed's report and states there are no third-party beneficiaries of its arrangements. That permission cannot be assumed to extend to this app's public archived datasets and exports. A precise redistribution grant and successful exact-download/schema review are still required; no observations were admitted.

Decision: no market-implied path panel, interpolated probabilities or placeholder is shipped. Existing `policyTargetLower`, `policyTargetUpper`, `iorb`, `effr` and `sofr` describe administered bounds or observed overnight rates; they are not relabeled market expectations. The exact remaining dependency is a permitted machine-readable price/probability source with public storage/export rights and explicit contract/observation/as-of dates, plus a reviewed transformation separating risk-neutral market pricing from a literal forecast. This is a rights/definition dependency, not a claim that another user's API key or an account would automatically solve it.
